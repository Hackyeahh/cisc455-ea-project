# CISC455/851 - Group Project Description

## Overview and Objectives:

The goal of this group project is to provide hands-on experience in designing, implementing, and reasoning about evolutionary algorithms for application problems. The project focuses on algorithmic design and behaviour, rather than extensive performance optimization for formal reporting.
Through this project, students will:

- Identify an application problem suitable for evolutionary optimization or learning
- Design an evolutionary algorithm tailored to the problem
- Implement a working prototype of the algorithm
- Observe and reflect on algorithm behaviour and limitations
- Clearly present and defend their design decisions

## Project Scope and Expectations:

Each group is expected to complete the following:

- Application Problem
  - Choose a real-world-motivated or practically inspired problem
  - The problem should be clearly defined and small-scale
  - Examples include (but are not limited to): scheduling, routing or resource allocation, symbolic regression, parameter tuning, combinatorial optimization, and games
- Justification for Using an EA
  - Explain why an EA is a reasonable or interesting choice
  - Discuss relevant problem characteristics (e.g., dimensionality, search space size, constraints, nonlinearity, variable interactions)
- Evolutionary Algorithm Design
  - Representation (genotype and phenotype)
  - Fitness evaluation
  - Variation operators (mutation and recombination)
  - Selection and population management
  - Parameter choices
- Implementation
  - Implement a working EA
  - Correctness and clarity matter more than performance
  - Experiments and results (illustrative rather than extensive)
- Reflection
  - What worked as expected?
  - What limitations or failure modes were observed?
  - What design choices mattered most?

## Project Presentation and Defense:

Each group will give an in-class project presentation, which serves as a mini project defense.

- Length: 6-7 minutes, followed by a Q&A
- Focus:
  - Problem formulation
  - EA design
  - Key implementation details
  - Observed behaviour and limitations

The presentation should demonstrate understanding and reasoning, not polished performance or exhaustive evaluation.

## What to Submit:

Slides and code (with a short README) to OnQ to support verification of the work presented.

## Evaluation:

The project will be graded at the group level based on the following criteria. Individual student's grade will be adjusted based on peer evaluations.

- Appropriateness and clarity of the problem formulation
- Soundness of evolutionary algorithm design
- Quality of the implementation as a working prototype
- Insightfulness of observations and limitations
- Effectiveness of the presentation and defense

For each criterium, the following rubric applies

| Level 4-Excellent<br>10 points | Level 3-Good<br>8 points | Level 2-Satisfactory<br>6 points | Level 1–Needs improv.<br>4 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| The aspect is addressed clearly, correctly, and thoughtfully. Design choices are well motivated, appropriate for the problem, and demonstrate a strong understanding of evolutionary algorithm principles. Limitations or trade-offs are acknowledged where relevant. | The aspect is addressed correctly and reasonably well. The design or explanation is sound but may lack depth, clarity, or full justification. Some design choices are implicit rather than explicitly motivated. | The aspect is partially addressed. The basic idea is present, but the design or explanation is superficial, incomplete, or loosely connected to the problem. Important details, justification, or reasoning are missing or unclear. | The aspect is poorly addressed or largely missing. The design or explanation shows significant misunderstanding, lacks coherence, or does not meaningfully relate to evolutionary algorithm concepts. |

## Presentation Commentary:

As part of the project presentations, each student is required to submit brief written commentaries on the presentations delivered by **four other groups, with one commentary submitted per group**.

- Students are encouraged to structure their presentation commentaries with reference to the project evaluation criteria. The purpose is to guide constructive and focused feedback, **not to assign scores or grades**.
- This activity is intended to encourage attentive participation, critical thinking, and engagement with different design choices across projects. Presentation commentaries are assessed as an individual class participation component. Submitted commentaries must be **anonymized**, as they may be shared with the presenting groups for feedback purposes.
